# Problem Set 01 - Solutions

## Task 01 - Gauss's Law

### Problem Statement

A point charge of $+2 \mathrm{C}$ is located at the origin. The electric flux through a spherical surface of radius $1 \mathrm{m}$ centered at the origin must be calculated.

### Theory

Gauss's law relates the total electric flux through a closed surface to the total charge enclosed by that surface.

$$
\Phi_E = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

where:

- $\Phi_E$ is the electric flux through the closed surface,
- $Q_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is the total charge enclosed by the surface,
- $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity.

The value of the vacuum permittivity is

$$
\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \frac{\mathrm{C}^2}{\mathrm{N} \cdot \mathrm{m}^2}
$$

For a point charge located at the center of a spherical Gaussian surface, the electric field has the same magnitude everywhere on the sphere and is directed radially outward. However, Gauss's law allows the flux to be found directly from the enclosed charge without first calculating the electric field.

### Step-by-Step Solution

The charge is located at the origin, and the spherical surface is centered at the origin. Therefore, the point charge lies inside the closed spherical surface.

The enclosed charge is

$$
Q_{\mathrm{enc}} = +2 \mathrm{C}
$$

According to Gauss's law,

$$
\Phi_E = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

Substitute the known values:

$$
\Phi_E = \frac{2}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}}
$$

Calculate the numerical value:

$$
\Phi_E \approx 2.26 \times 10^{11}
$$

The SI unit of electric flux is

$$
\frac{\mathrm{N} \cdot \mathrm{m}^2}{\mathrm{C}}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\Phi_E \approx 2.26 \times 10^{11} \frac{\mathrm{N} \cdot \mathrm{m}^2}{\mathrm{C}}
$$

### Final Result

$$
\Phi_E \approx 2.26 \times 10^{11} \frac{\mathrm{N} \cdot \mathrm{m}^2}{\mathrm{C}}
$$

### Interpretation

The electric flux is positive because the enclosed charge is positive. A positive point charge produces electric field lines directed outward, so the net flux through the closed spherical surface is outward.

The radius of the sphere does not affect the total electric flux. According to Gauss's law, the total flux depends only on the total charge enclosed by the closed surface.
